Method and sources

Every figure on this page traces to the committed snapshot, which records its source, license and retrieval date for each value.

Sourcing rule

Every figure on this page is fetched by scripts/snapshot.mjs from the cited open data sources and stored with provenance (source name, table or dataset, license, retrieval date) in src/data/snapshot.json. Nothing statistical is hand-entered, except the DJI block, which is committed by hand in the script and labeled with its source, document, version and data year. Derived values (per-year costs, per-100 funnel stages, occupancy, probabilities) are computed at render time from snapshot fields and flagged as derived; derived status chips are computed from the snapshot series, never hand-written. Tooltips repeat figures already sourced and linked on the card; they introduce no new sources.

Unit table

  • Crimes: registered crimes (misdrijven), counts and per 1,000 inhabitants.
  • Suspects: registrations of suspects (verdachten), counts and per 10,000 inhabitants.
  • Cases: prosecutor decisions and court verdicts, counts.
  • Sanctions: imposed sanctions, counts and shares.
  • Detainees: persons in prison (stock on 30 September, or annual flow), counts.
  • Victims: murder and manslaughter victims, counts and per 1,000,000 inhabitants.
  • Eurostat: persons held and official capacity, counts and per 100,000 inhabitants.

Rates, counts and the population register

Suspect rates are per 10,000 within each group's own population, so a group rate is a weighted average of its subgroup rates, never their sum. CBS counts include suspects who are not in the municipal population register (GBA) while the rates do not, so counts and rates do not reconstruct each other exactly.

Chip rule

A status chip compares the latest value of a series against the value five years earlier (short term) and against the historical peak of the series (long term). The arrow and the percentage in five years come from the short-term comparison; the below-peak percentage comes from the long-term one. Chips are computed at render time from the snapshot.

Per-card year ceilings

  • Registered crime series: through 2025 (CBS 83723NED, 78 years).
  • Suspects by age, sex, background, offence: 2022 (CBS 81947NED).
  • Prosecutor decisions, verdicts, sanctions: 2022 (CBS 83944NED, 2001 to 2022).
  • Recidivism split: 2009 (CBS 81997NED; later years are suppressed).
  • Background and generation: 2022 (CBS 81959NED).
  • Education gradient: 2024, income gradient: 2024 (CBS 85658NED; the newest year's breakdowns are suppressed).
  • Detainee characteristics: 2025 (CBS 85836NED).
  • 30 September stock: through 2025 (CBS 85841NED).
  • Murder and manslaughter victims: through 2025 (CBS 84726NED).
  • Eurostat prison capacity and persons held: 2024 (crim_pris_cap).
  • DJI costs and capacity: data year 2025, May 2026.

Suppression rendering rule

CBS suppresses some cells (shown as "." in StatLine). The snapshot stores them as null, and the page renders them as gaps with an "n/a, suppressed" note, never as zero.

Age-structure caveat

Age-specific suspect rates are CBS's own rates per 10,000 inhabitants of that age group. The youngest band (12 to 18 years) spans only six years of age, so its rate moves more sharply than the underlying counts; read it as a peak shape, not a precise level.

The justice categorical tokens (four funnel stages, seven offence categories, seven stacked-segment tokens) are defined per theme in app/globals.css. Every token clears 3 to 1 against both surfaces of its theme (page and card, light and dark), and every adjacent pair of tokens clears 3 to 1 against each other; the full ratio table is documented in the stylesheet comment.
